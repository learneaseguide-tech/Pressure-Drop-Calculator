Conclusion and recommendations

Method and assumptions

  • Darcy–Weisbach friction with the Colebrook–White friction factor; 64/Re below Re 2,000, with a linear blend through the transitional band up to Re 4,000.
  • Compressible flow is treated as isothermal at the stated temperature, using the average line pressure for density and velocity (the standard average-density method). With an outlet temperature entered, density uses the mean of the inlet and outlet temperatures, temperature is taken to vary linearly along the line, and the reported velocities use the actual temperature at each point. Accuracy falls off once the drop passes about 40% of the inlet pressure; split the line into segments then.
  • Fitting losses are ΣK on the average velocity head, plus the elevation term. ΣK is the sum of K × quantity over the fittings you add; the built-in K values are typical turbulent-flow figures for full-size fittings.
  • Gas density comes from the ideal-gas law with the Z you enter. Use "Inlet density" for saturated steam or dense-phase gas.
  • Choking is assessed at M = 1/√k, the limit for isothermal flow.
  • Liquids are taken as incompressible at constant density. Vapour pressure and cavitation are not checked, so compare the outlet pressure with the fluid's vapour pressure.
